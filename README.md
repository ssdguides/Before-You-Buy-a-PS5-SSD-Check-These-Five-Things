The PS5 doesn’t give you much space. The original console ships with 825 GB, and after the system takes its share you’re left with around 667 GB. A few large games plus patches fill that quickly, which is why many players add an internal M.2 SSD.

But the PS5 is picky. Here’s what to check.

1. The interface. It must be an M.2 NVMe drive with an M-key connector running PCIe Gen4 x4. M.2 SATA drives aren’t supported.

2. The speed filter. Sony recommends at least 5,500 MB/s sequential read. Nearly every Gen4 gaming SSD clears that, so treat it as a filter, not a ranking. For many players, paying extra for a higher advertised speed may not make a noticeable difference in gameplay.

3. The cooling fit. Sony requires an effective heat-dissipation solution, and the whole assembly must stay within about 11.25 mm in total thickness. If the slot cover won’t close, the cooler likely exceeds the limits, so stop and check rather than forcing it.

4. The cache design. This is the check many guides skip. Some drives carry their own DRAM. Others, called HMB drives, borrow part of the host device’s memory. Sony says the PS5 doesn’t support HMB and that those drives may be slower than expected. The guide prefers onboard-DRAM drives when prices are close, and treats the Lexar NM790, an HMB drive, as a budget option with a caveat. It says plainly that it hasn’t found a Sony measurement of the slowdown.

5. The capacity. Add up the install sizes of games you play monthly, then add 40 to 50 percent for patches and new releases, and round up. Compare the price per terabyte of the sizes either side.

Then decide on internal vs external. An external USB SSD can store PS5 games but not run them, so it’s an archive, not an expansion.

Sony also says it can’t guarantee that every drive meeting its specification will work in every console, and that an expansion drive may behave differently in some games.
